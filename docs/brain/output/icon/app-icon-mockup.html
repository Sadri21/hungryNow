HungryNow — App Icon

Revised twice on 2026-09-08. First because the original mark — a location pin with one concentric arc — carried no food signal at all, which was a hole in the reasoning rather than a trade-off worth keeping: portfolio-art-direction.md Decision 3 justified the pin with “it says find a place near me, which is what the app does”, and the app does not find places, it answers one button that says I'm Hungry. Then again to carry all three ideas Sadri asked for — food, time, place — in a single mark.

Three components — it works, with one argument against

It fits, and it survives 29pt. That was the open question and the answer is yes: food in the tines, time in the clock counter, place in the taper to a point. The clock is legible all the way down because it sits in the body's widest part, where there is room for a 256-unit face.

The argument against is not technical, it's redundancy. The icon never appears without the word HungryNow directly beneath it. “Now” is already said, in letters, by the label — so a third of the icon's complexity budget goes to restating the one component the user can already read. Food and place are not in the name. That is the whole case for two components, and it is a strategy call rather than a drawing problem, so it's Sadri's to make.

Asked for · three components

Fork · clock · pin

Tines, a clock counter and a pin taper in one path. Busiest of the set and the only one that carries all three ideas. Thinnest feature is the 42-unit clock hand — 1.19pt at 29pt, which now sets the whole icon's floor.

Two components

Fork-pin

The same mark with the clock removed. Food and place fused, nothing else — and no counter at all, so nothing can go opaque-wrong in the tinted and clear variants. This is the version that survives being shrunk, cropped or badly reproduced.

Food-forward

Bowl & ping

Most food-legible mark drawn and the safest small — two fat forms, nothing fine. The arc doubles as steam and as screen 05's radar sweep. Cost: “near me” is gone.

Superseded

Pin & arc

The 2026-09-07 decision, kept as the fallback. Clear, well-built, and says nothing about food — it would sit unremarked among a hundred nearby-discovery apps.

The constraint the whole set came from: detail small enough to sit inside a pin's counter is around 2pt at 29pt and vanishes — which is why the food signal had to move into the silhouette, and why the clock had to go in the body rather than the head. Nothing here is inside the old counter, because nothing can be.

Only the fills change — HIG forbids swapping core elements between variants, so there is no “simpler dark version”. Check the clear tile carefully. The clock is a real hole in the artwork, and every hole is a place where the system's own recolouring shows through; the two-component fork-pin has no counter at all, which is the quiet reason it is the more robust mark. The tinted and clear tiles here are approximations — the real ones are system-generated from the layered Icon Composer file, and iOS 27 composites extra Liquid Glass layers into the artwork itself.

Drawn and rejected — do not redraw these

Bowl as the pin's counter

A flat-topped void inside a circle is a mouth. The pin becomes a face.

Bite out of the pin

Reads as two overlapping blobs, not a bite — and invites an Apple-logo comparison.

Bowl with the arc touching

The arc becomes a handle and the mark is a picnic basket — the grocery-app trap Decision 3 rejected the pomegranate for.

Fork-pin with a flared belly

Flaring below the tine base turns the silhouette into an arrowhead. The widest point must be the shoulder itself.

Two more that died the same way and aren't worth a tile: a bowl tapering to a point reads as a shield, and cutting tine slots into a round pin head starves the outer tines to ~28 units because the circle curves away from them. The fork head has to be a flat-topped block; that is a geometric fact, not a style preference.

Build notes & what's still open

Icon Composer

  • Two layers, not a flat PNG. Background = solid blush; foreground = the fork-pin SVG. Layering is what earns the system-generated variants and the Liquid Glass highlights.
  • Import the SVG, don't re-trace it. Every dimension in the table above is tuned to the 29pt threshold; a hand-drawn version will not be.
  • Crisp edges only. No shadow, no feather, no inner glow — soft edges break the system-drawn specular highlights.
  • The outline is one continuous subpath tracing tines and body together, deliberately. The first attempt drew the tines as separate rectangles overrunning the body, and under evenodd those overlaps became holes — white slivers across every tine join. Don't reintroduce the overlap.
  • The clock hands are one connected L polygon, also deliberately. Two capsules meeting at the clock's centre overlap there, and that overlap punches a hole straight through the middle of the face.
  • Every corner is filleted (2026-09-09), and that is not a HIG conflict. “Crisp edges only” forbids blur and feathering, not curvature — a rounded corner is still a crisp edge, and visual-identity.md already asks for rounded rendering. Notch floors became full semicircular U's (the biggest single gain: square notches read as slots, U's read as a fork), hand ends and elbows got caps, and the pin tip got an r 34 fillet. r 48 was tested and rejected — at 48 the tip reads as a teardrop and the “place” signal goes.
  • Content is a 390×600 box centred on a 1024 canvas, so corner masking can't truncate it.

Open / verify

  • Pick two components or three. Three is drawn and works; the case against it is redundancy with the word “HungryNow” sitting under the icon, not feasibility.
  • Confirm the 29pt row on a real device. A browser at 29 CSS pixels is not a Settings list.
  • iOS 27 changed icon guidance for the second year running — check Apple's live Icon Composer docs before shipping. apple-hig.md is stale on exactly this point and says so.
  • If the fork-pin is adopted, portfolio-art-direction.md Decision 3 needs its revision recorded, not just this file.
  • The pomegranate is not revived by this. It references the palette's name, which nobody outside the vault knows, and reads as a grocery app.
